\section{RL Agents and Results}

\subsection{Algorithm Choice}

The multi-group environment has a \texttt{MultiDiscrete} action space of size $26^8 \approx 2 \times 10^{11}$, making value-based methods like DQN infeasible since they require enumerating all actions. We therefore use \textbf{Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)}~\cite{schulman2017ppo}, a policy-gradient method that handles large, structured action spaces naturally.

For the single-group (\texttt{O+}) variant with \texttt{Discrete(26)} actions, we additionally train \textbf{DQN}~\cite{mnih2015dqn} to compare value-based and policy-gradient approaches. All implementations use Stable-Baselines3~\cite{raffin2021sb3}.

\subsection{Discussion}

The RL agent must balance two opposing costs: ordering too much leads to expiry (perishable platelets have a 5-day shelf life), while ordering too little causes shortages. The base-stock rule achieves this via a fixed target proportional to mean demand, but cannot adapt to stochastic surges or weekday patterns beyond what the static $k$ captures.

PPO has access to the full observation---current stock levels across all shelf-life columns, the order pipeline, 7 days of demand history, and the day of week---allowing it to learn \emph{state-dependent} ordering. In particular, it can reduce orders before weekends (lower demand) and increase them before high-demand weekdays.

\textbf{Substitution.} In multi-group mode, compatible blood types can substitute for each other (e.g., O$-$ for any group). This adds a buffer that reduces shortages for rare groups (AB$-$, O$-$) but complicates inventory planning since donors' stock may be depleted by recipients.

\textbf{Single-group comparison.} DQN and PPO perform comparably on the single-group task, as expected for a small discrete action space.

\subsection{Limitations}

Our simulator, while calibrated from published statistics~\cite{abouee2023platelet, uclh2024platelet}, uses synthetic demand rather than real clinical data. The model assumes a fixed 1-day lead time, and platelet ABO compatibility is simplified to red-cell substitution rules. Future work could incorporate variable lead times, crossmatch constraints, and transfer learning from real hospital data.

\subsection{Conclusion}

We demonstrate that PPO can learn effective platelet ordering policies in a realistic simulated blood bank environment, competing with or improving upon a carefully tuned base-stock heuristic. The open-source codebase supports reproducible evaluation across 30 seeds, and all results are generated by the committed code.
