\begin{abstract}
We formulate platelet inventory management in a hospital blood bank as a Markov decision process and train reinforcement learning agents (PPO, DQN) to minimise a composite cost of shortages, wastage from expiry, and holding costs. Demand is simulated via a negative binomial model calibrated from published hospital statistics. We compare trained RL policies against random, mean-demand, and base-stock baselines on 30 evaluation seeds over 365-day episodes. Results show that PPO learns competitive ordering strategies in both multi-group (8 blood types with substitution) and single-group settings.
\end{abstract}
